All scores are unclipped normalized scores, with task-level reference returns shared by every method and condition.

Gain over the base algorithm and over standard RL pool

Ten robust methods share a base algorithm (PPO, SAC or IQL) with a standard RL reference. On the library-wide grid of 220 configurations, each of them is compared with its own base algorithm, which shows what the mechanism adds, and with the standard RL pool of its regime, which also contains the strength of the base learner.

Online, the gain comes from the mechanism. ATLA-PPO, ATLA-SA-PPO and DR-SAC gain +7.2, +13.4 and +10.1 over their base algorithm. Online mechanisms train in the environment, so a policy that fails under a perturbation is penalized by its true return and the generated experience is real.

Offline, the gain comes mostly from the base learner. The five IQL-based methods are +10.1 to +14.0 above the offline standard RL pool but at most +3.8 above IQL. Offline mechanisms must generate such experience from a fixed dataset with a learned model, with no environment feedback to correct it. Additionally, offline algorithms update conservatively to limit value overestimation, which constrains the gain any mechanism can add.

Retention across shift severity

Retention decays at a similar rate overall, serving as a complementary metric against the absolute score. Figure 2 shows standard and robust RL, online and offline, and all five families share similar parallel retention curves. Online robust methods retain more than online standard methods at every level, so their mechanisms preserve nominal performance under shift. Offline robust methods retain about as much as offline standard methods (within 0.05 at every level) despite a positive absolute gap (Figure 1), so offline robustness is realized through higher nominal scores. Online, the data based family (environment-centric) retains less than the learner-centric family at every level, consistent with data based mechanisms that may augment more qualified data to improve nominal performance. Offline, the data-centric and learner-centric families retain about the same.

This comparison matches the training budget of ATLA-PPO, ATLA-SA-PPO, RAMBO and RORL to that of the standard RL references and reports RFQI at its last checkpoint, as every other method. Everything else is unchanged.

What changes

  • Robust against standard RL. The pooled gap is +6.7 (52.4 against 45.7), where the original setup has +7.9. It stays positive in every severity quartile. Most of the decrease is offline: the offline gap moves from +9.5 to +7.7, the online gap from +3.8 to +3.5.
  • Gain over the base algorithm. ATLA-PPO and ATLA-SA-PPO both gain +9.3 over PPO (+7.2 and +13.4 originally). The other gains of Figure 1 are unchanged.
  • Retention. The pattern is the same. Online robust methods retain more than online standard RL at every level, by 0.02 to 0.07, and offline robust methods retain as much as offline standard RL, within 0.03.
  • Leaders of the isolated shifts. Three leaders change. Online, DR-SAC now leads action (82.9) and reward (94.1), where ATLA-SA-PPO scores 61.7 and 73.5 (83.3 and 95.7 originally). Offline, RORL passes PLR-PVL on dynamics (46.6 against 45.8).
  • Gain per shift source. The largest gain over a base algorithm, ATLA-SA-PPO on observation, moves from +67 to +52 points. Every offline difference to IQL stays within 7 points.
  • Compound shifts. The offline method of the compound test is now FWM, because RORL is no longer the strongest offline method. Dynamics remain the worst single source (27.9 on Hopper, 51.3 on Pusher), and under composition both methods fall below a third of their nominal score (16.9 and 29.2).

The conclusions of the first two sections hold in this comparison.
